## What the measurements actually explain

The completed **cold historical** observation took 1481.386 wall seconds,
1392.423 self CPU seconds, with 1276.024 seconds in collection. It scanned
1,048,674,112 positions over 8001 polynomials, admitted 24,367 atoms and retained
4992 rows. Its 65,508,038 refined rejections occur before ordinary prime division;
they are not 65 million failed cofactor-splitting attempts. Summed plan-byte
gauges are not peak resident memory. This one observation does not measure
current mainline or establish a population-wide 60-digit limit.

Identical-work calibration measured observer overhead separately. The queue
prefixes then changed throughput while matching candidates, rejection, root-hit,
power-hit, admission and state hashes. Roughly 76–77% of sparse prime/block
visits were empty in the census. This supports eliminating repeated empty
visits; it does not establish a useful-yield improvement or explain every
remaining collection cost. Separate root timing in the historical call was
29.648 seconds and elimination 1.846 seconds. Those measurements limit the
immediate priority of base-root and solver work, without predicting larger sizes.

The useful-yield questions remain real. Native nominal score bounds can differ
from recovered-product admission; [Yamaquasi's actual cofactor caller](https://github.com/remyoudompheng/yamaquasi/blob/270a66177cc10ab52d49ea4f371a5e1f69664b4b/src/fbase.rs)
illustrates that distinction. [Ekkelkamp §§3.3.2–3.3.3](https://math.leidenuniv.nl/scripties/EkkelkampThesis.pdf)
studies unequal ordered endpoint bounds and shows that maximum relation density
need not minimize time. These are different C9a interventions. More raw atoms,
mixed dependency touches or early face rows are not causal evidence of a gain.
